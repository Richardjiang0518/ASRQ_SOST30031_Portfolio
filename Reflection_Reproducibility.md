# Why reproducibility matters in social research

Reproducibility is not a technical detail in social research; it is the
foundation on which causal claims earn trust. Unlike laboratory
sciences, social research informs policy directly — on education,
poverty, and health — so a single unreproducible study can influence
millions of lives.

A famous example is the correlation between ice cream sales and
drowning deaths. The two rise together every summer, yet neither
causes the other: hot weather drives both. Without careful analysis,
a researcher could "discover" that ice cream is deadly. Reproducible
methods — clear data, documented code, explicit assumptions — are
what allow others to check whether a pattern reflects a real effect
or a hidden confounder.


Version control with Git embodies the remedy. Every change to an
analysis becomes a recorded, inspectable commit, allowing others to
trace exactly how a conclusion was produced. This portfolio is my
first step towards making my own work reproducible by default.
